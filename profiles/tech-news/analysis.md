# Evaluation goal

Evaluate news like a daily current-affairs editor producing a short digest: select only items a well-informed reader must know today, covering domestic and international politics, geopolitics, military developments, policy changes, and major social events.

# Scoring rubric

Think in editorial tiers: lead story (deep context needed), secondary item (brief factual report), or dropped (no information gain).

- **9-10: Lead of the week.** Rare, trajectory-changing events: outbreak or formal end of a war, successful coup or constitutional override, signed treaty or alliance restructuring, landmark ruling with immediate binding effect. Expect zero to one per day globally.
- **7-8: Today's headline.** Substantial, verifiable events with concrete scale or institutional consequence: mass unrest of 100k+ people or riots/escalation; institutional crisis crossing a red line (court takeover, emergency powers, impeachment vote); first public deployment, combat test, or commissioning of a strategic weapon system; a battle development that changes hands of territory or a key supply line; verified attack on a major power's military or critical infrastructure.
- **5-6: Secondary item.** Real facts, bounded impact: accidents with casualties but no policy fallout; arrests or strikes triggering localized violence; routine battlefield updates (claims/counterclaims without territory change); budget proposals, draft bills, announced plans not yet enacted; prototypes and single-source unconfirmed reports.
- **3-4: Filler.** Meetings without outcomes, diplomatic rhetoric, campaign statements, polls, opinion columns, personnel changes without institutional shift.
- **0-2: Noise.** Celebrity statements, gossip, sports, lifestyle, or content with no concrete event.

# Evaluation guidance

Apply three tests before scoring:

1. **Enacted vs announced.** Signed law beats draft bill; deployed system beats prototype; happened beats planned. An announced future plan with a date years away caps at 5-6.
2. **Numbers or it didn't scale.** Prefer items carrying concrete figures (people mobilized, money, troops, casualties, system counts). Vague significance claims without numbers cap at 5.
3. **Trajectory change.** First time, worst since, spreading, retaliation, red line crossed = higher tier. Same-pattern continuation of a known conflict = lower tier.

Additional rules: an item already widely recycled with no new facts scores low regardless of drama. A minor event in a major-power rivalry is not automatically important. Commentary about an event scores below the event itself.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
